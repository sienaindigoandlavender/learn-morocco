# Romans in Africa

Rome ruled some part of the African continent continuously for roughly seven and a half centuries — from the destruction of Carthage in 146 BCE to the Arab conquest of Egypt in 641 CE — and at peak (the 2nd century CE) controlled an unbroken coastal arc from the Atlantic at Sala to the Red Sea at Berenice. Seven provinces, two African-born emperors (Septimius Severus and his son Caracalla), perhaps a quarter of the imperial grain supply, three of the most influential Latin Christian thinkers (Tertullian, Cyprian, Augustine), and one of the great archaeological landscapes of the Roman world.

## Phoenician Prelude (c. 1100 BCE – 264 BCE)

Rome's African story is a long aftermath of Carthage's. Phoenician traders from Tyre settle the North African coast from the late 12th century BCE — Utica, Lixus, Hadrumetum, Lepcis, and at last Carthage itself, traditionally founded in 814 BCE. By the 6th century the Carthaginian state controls the coast from western Libya to the Atlantic, with substantial inland reach into Numidia. The Greek colonization of Cyrene (631 BCE) and Egypt under the Ptolemies (305 BCE – 30 BCE) sets up the eastern half of what will become Roman Africa.

## The Punic Wars and the Province of Africa (264 – 27 BCE)

- **264–241 BCE [First Punic War]** — Rome and Carthage fight for Sicily.
- **218–201 BCE [Second Punic War]** — Hannibal's Italian campaign; Scipio's invasion of Africa; the battle of Zama (202 BCE).
- **149–146 BCE [Third Punic War]** — Cato's *delenda est Carthago*. Carthage destroyed by Scipio Aemilianus in 146 BCE; the territory becomes the Roman province of **Africa** (later Africa Vetus). Capital initially at Utica.
- **111–105 BCE [Jugurthine War]** — Rome fights Jugurtha of Numidia. Marius and Sulla make their reputations. Bocchus I of Mauretania betrays Jugurtha to end the war.
- **96 BCE** — Ptolemy Apion bequeaths Cyrene to Rome.
- **74 BCE** — Cyrenaica organized as the province of **Creta et Cyrenaica**.
- **46 BCE** — Caesar defeats the Pompeian holdouts (and Juba I of Numidia) at Thapsus. Numidia annexed as the province of **Africa Nova**, later merged with Africa Vetus into **Africa Proconsularis**.
- **30 BCE** — Octavian defeats Mark Antony and Cleopatra VII at Actium; Egypt annexed as the imperial province of **Aegyptus**, governed by an equestrian *praefectus Aegypti* — senators forbidden to enter without imperial permission.

## The High Empire (27 BCE – 235 CE)

- **27 BCE – 14 CE [Augustus]** — Veteran colonies founded across Africa Proconsularis (Carthage refounded 29 BCE). Veteran colonies in Mauretania (Zilil, Babba, Banasa) seeded 33 BCE.
- **25 BCE** — Augustus installs **Juba II** as client king of Mauretania, with capitals at Caesarea (Cherchell) and Volubilis. The arrangement holds for sixty-five years.
- **19 BCE** — Cornelius Balbus campaigns into the Fezzan and reaches the Garamantian capital at Garama — the deepest Roman penetration into the Sahara. Returns with rare wood to Rome's first non-imperial triumph.
- **17–24 CE [Tacfarinas]** — The Numidian Tacfarinas, a Roman auxiliary deserter, leads a long Berber revolt across Africa Proconsularis. Suppressed under Tiberius.
- **40–44 CE [Mauretania annexed]** — Caligula has Ptolemy of Mauretania assassinated. After the Aedemon revolt, Claudius creates **Mauretania Tingitana** and **Mauretania Caesariensis**.
- **70 CE** — *Legio III Augusta* permanently based at Theveste; later (c. 81 CE) moved to **Lambaesis** in Numidia, where it stays for two and a half centuries.
- **96–117 CE [Trajan]** — **Thamugadi** (Timgad) founded as a veteran colony in Numidia: the textbook gridded Roman city. Cuicul (Djemila) founded under Nerva.
- **115–117 CE [Kitos War]** — The Jewish revolt under Trajan devastates Cyrenaica and Egypt. Cyrene takes generations to recover.
- **130 CE [Hadrian]** — Hadrian visits Africa, addresses the troops at Lambaesis (the speech survives, inscribed). Founds **Antinooüpolis** in Egypt after Antinous drowns in the Nile.
- **193–211 CE [Septimius Severus]** — Severus, born at Lepcis Magna, becomes emperor. Massive Severan building program at Lepcis and across Africa Proconsularis. Caracalla (born at Lugdunum but Severan-African on his father's side) issues the *Constitutio Antoniniana* in 212 CE — universal Roman citizenship across the empire.
- **c. 197–202 CE** — Severus campaigns at the southern frontier, extending the Tripolitanian *limes* deep into the pre-desert.
- **2nd–3rd c.** — The African economic peak. Olive oil from Numidia and Tripolitania; wheat from Africa Proconsularis and Egypt. Carthage is the second city of the Latin West. African Latin literature flourishes (Apuleius, Tertullian, Fronto).

## The Crisis and the Reorganization (235 – 410 CE)

- **238 CE** — The 'Year of the Six Emperors' begins in Africa with the proclamation of Gordian I and II at Thysdrus. Both die within weeks.
- **253–268 CE** — Crisis of the Third Century. Mauri raids; *Legio III Augusta* disbanded by Gallienus c. 238 (briefly), reformed under Valerian.
- **285–297 CE [Diocletian]** — Reorganization. Africa Proconsularis split into **Zeugitana**, **Byzacena**, **Tripolitania**, plus **Numidia Cirtensis** and **Numidia Militiana**. Mauretania split into **Tingitana**, **Caesariensis**, **Sitifensis**. Mauretania Tingitana withdrawn to a northern strip and attached to Hispania.
- **303–305 CE [Great Persecution]** — Diocletian's persecutions hit African Christianity hard. Origins of the **Donatist** schism, which will define African Christianity for the next century and a half.
- **312–313 CE [Constantine]** — Edict of Milan. Donatist controversy formally adjudicated against the rigorists; the schism nevertheless persists.
- **354–430 CE [Augustine]** — Augustine of Hippo, born in Numidia, bishop of Hippo Regius from 396, dies during the Vandal siege of his city. His century is the apex of Latin Christian thought in Africa.

## Vandal Africa (429 – 533 CE)

- **429 CE** — The Vandals and Alans under Genseric cross from Spain at Tingis (perhaps 80,000 people) and march east. Hippo falls 431; Carthage 439.
- **439–533 CE** — The **Vandal Kingdom of Africa**, with capital at Carthage. Arian Christianity imposed on a Nicene population. Vandal navy raids the Mediterranean (sack of Rome, 455).
- **484 CE** — Vandal persecution under Huneric.

## Byzantine Africa (533 – c. 700 CE)

- **533–534 CE [Belisarius]** — Justinian's general Belisarius reconquers Vandal Africa in a single campaigning season. Re-established as the **Praetorian Prefecture of Africa**, raised to **Exarchate of Carthage** under Maurice c. 590.
- **mid-6th c.** — Long counter-insurgency against Berber kingdoms (the *Mauri* now reorganized into independent kingdoms in the Aurès, the Hodna, and Mauretania).
- **609–610 CE** — The Exarch Heraclius the Elder revolts from Carthage; his son Heraclius sails for Constantinople, deposes Phocas, and becomes emperor.
- **619–629 CE** — Sasanian Persia briefly conquers Egypt; recovered by Heraclius.
- **641–642 CE** — **Arab conquest of Egypt** under Amr ibn al-As. Alexandria falls 642 (briefly retaken by Byzantines 645, finally lost 646).
- **647 CE [Sufetula]** — First Arab raid into Byzacena. The Exarch Gregory killed at the **Battle of Sufetula**. Carthage holds for another half-century.
- **670 CE** — Foundation of **Kairouan** by Uqba ibn Nafi as the Arab base for the conquest of the Maghreb.
- **698 CE** — Hassan ibn al-Nu'man takes **Carthage**, the great Roman-Byzantine capital, and dismantles its harbor. End of the Roman presence in Africa Proconsularis.
- **c. 711 CE** — Septem (Ceuta), the last Roman-Byzantine outpost on the southern shore of the strait, falls. Tariq ibn Ziyad crosses to Iberia. → [[al-andalus]], [[andalus-maghreb-connections]]

## Threads Worth Following

- **The grain economy.** Egypt and Africa Proconsularis between them fed Rome (and later Constantinople). The political economy of the empire is unintelligible without them.
- **African Christianity.** The Latin Christian tradition is largely an African invention — Tertullian (Carthage), Cyprian (Carthage), Augustine (Hippo), the Donatist movement, the early martyr-acts. → [[augustine-of-hippo]]
- **The African emperors.** Septimius Severus and the Severan dynasty are the watershed: an African ruling house remakes Rome's monumental and legal landscape. → [[severans]]
- **The Berber substrate.** Mauri / Numidian / Garamantian polities are not extras to the Roman story — they are the population, before, during, and after. → [[amazigh-timeline]]
- **The Arab conquest.** From Egypt 641 to Ceuta c. 711, the Roman-Byzantine arc collapses in seventy years. → [[arab-conquest]], [[al-andalus]]
