# Architectural Timeline of Morocco

Buildings outlast dynasties, and they outlast the stories told about dynasties. What follows is a parallel chronology to [[timeline-of-morocco]] — same country, same centuries, but cut through what's still standing rather than who was in charge. Where the political timeline asks *who ruled?*, this one asks *what did they build, and what's left of it?*

A building is on this list if it is dated, locatable, and either still standing or substantively visible in ruin. Where the most-cited building of a period has been so heavily restored that what you see is nineteenth- or twentieth-century, that's noted.

For the techniques and ornamental vocabularies that move across periods see [[andalusi-inheritance]], [[zellij]], [[muqarnas]], [[tadelakt]], and [[earthen-architecture-morocco]].

## Pre-Islamic Substrate (before c. 700 CE)

- **c. 800 BCE [Phoenician]** — Trading post at Lixus on the Atlantic. Earliest stratified urbanism in Morocco. Under the later Roman levels.
- **c. 500 BCE [Phoenician]** — Mogador / Essaouira island settlement. Punic murex-purple production. Visible only as foundations.
- **c. 300 BCE – 40 CE [Mauretanian]** — Volubilis under the client-kings, including Juba II. The pre-Roman urban core.
- **c. 40 CE – 285 CE [Roman]** — [[volubilis]] expanded to its full plan: capitol, basilica, triumphal arch of Caracalla (217 CE), the great courtyard houses with mosaics. The most legible Roman city in Morocco.
- **c. 200 CE [Roman]** — Sala Colonia (later Chellah, Rabat). Roman forum, decumanus, baths. Now the underlayer of the [[chellah]] necropolis.
- **5th–7th c.** — Roman urbanism collapses. Volubilis continues as a small Christian-then-Berber town. Most other Roman sites abandoned.

## Early Islamic Morocco (700 – 1050)

- **789 [Idrisid]** — Foundation of [[fes]] by Idris I; expansion by Idris II from 808. The earliest continuously inhabited Islamic city in the country. The street network of [[fes-el-bali]] is essentially Idrisid in skeleton.
- **859 [Idrisid]** — [[al-qarawiyyin]] mosque-and-madrasa founded in Fes by Fatima al-Fihri. Continuously functioning since. Most of the visible fabric is Almoravid-and-later, but the foundation date stands.
- **859 [Idrisid]** — Andalusiyyin mosque founded in Fes by Maryam al-Fihri, on the Andalusian bank of the medina. Together with al-Qarawiyyin it sets the two-bank pattern of the city.
- **c. 900 [Idrisid]** — Walls of [[moulay-idriss-zerhoun]] around the tomb of Idris I. The pilgrimage geography of northern Morocco begins here.

## Almoravid Marrakesh (1062 – 1147)

- **1062 [Almoravid]** — Foundation of [[marrakesh]] by Yusuf ibn Tashfin. The new Saharan-imperial capital. The original walls and the Koubba Almoravid (the small ablutions pavilion) are the only surface survivals; the rest was overbuilt by the Almohads.
- **c. 1100 [Almoravid]** — The Koubba al-Barudiyyin in Marrakesh — the only intact Almoravid-period building in the city. Tiny, vaulted, and decisive: the ornamental vocabulary that the Almohads would scale up is already complete here.
- **1135 [Almoravid]** — Tlemcen Great Mosque (now Algeria, but Almoravid). Cited because its formal program is the immediate ancestor of the Almohad mosques in Marrakesh and Seville.
- **1126–1140 [Almoravid]** — Walls of Marrakesh. The original ramparts, in pisé (rammed earth), still define the medina perimeter.

## Almohad Imperial (1147 – 1269)

- **1147 [Almohad]** — Almohad takeover of Marrakesh; the Almoravid mosque is razed.
- **1153 [Almohad]** — [[tinmel-mosque]] in the High Atlas, founded by Abd al-Mu'min as the dynastic shrine. Ruined in the 2023 earthquake; now under restoration. The clearest surviving Almohad plan.
- **1158 [Almohad]** — First [[koutoubia]] in Marrakesh, mis-aligned. Demolished and replaced by the present mosque, c. 1195. The minaret is the prototype for Seville's Giralda (begun 1184) and Rabat's Hassan Tower (begun 1195).
- **1184–1199 [Almohad]** — Yacoub al-Mansour's building campaign. New imperial capital at [[rabat]]: the Kasbah of the Oudayas, the city walls (Bab er-Rouah, Bab Oudaia), and the unfinished Hassan Mosque with its 44-metre stub of a minaret.
- **1195 [Almohad]** — Bab Agnaou, the ceremonial gate of Marrakesh. The high-water mark of Almohad stone carving.
- **c. 1200 [Almohad]** — Tin Mal and Aghmat fortifications. The High Atlas military landscape that the dynasty grew out of.

## Marinid Refinement (1269 – 1465)

- **1276 [Marinid]** — Foundation of [[fes-el-jdid]] (New Fes) west of the old city by Abu Yusuf Yaqub. Royal palace, garrison quarter, and — from 1438 — the [[mellah]].
- **1323 [Marinid]** — [[al-attarine-madrasa]] in Fes. The first of the great Marinid madrasas.
- **1325 [Marinid]** — [[bou-inania-madrasa]] in Meknes.
- **1350–1356 [Marinid]** — [[bou-inania-madrasa|Bou Inania madrasa in Fes]], by Abu Inan. The peak of the Marinid madrasa form: zellij dado, carved cedar, stucco upper register, three-aisle prayer hall.
- **c. 1340 [Marinid]** — [[chellah]] necropolis at Rabat: minaret, sanctuary, garden walls, all built over the Roman Sala. Now the most photographed Marinid ruin.
- **c. 1350 [Marinid]** — Sahrij and Sba'iyin madrasas in Fes. Less famous but more intimate than Bou Inania.

## Wattasid Interregnum (1465 – 1554)

- **1438 [late Marinid]** — Foundation of the [[mellah]] in Fes el-Jdid. The walled-quarter institution begins here. (Listed under Marinid politically, Wattasid in physical aftermath.)
- **15th c. [Wattasid]** — Defensive rebuilding under Portuguese pressure on the coast. Asilah, El-Jadida (Mazagan from 1514), Safi, Azemmour all acquire Portuguese fortifications that are now their most visible historical layer.

## Saadian Gold (1554 – 1659)

- **1557–1574 [Saadian]** — [[ben-youssef-madrasa]] rebuilt in Marrakesh under Abdallah al-Ghalib. The largest madrasa in North Africa; the ornamental program is the bridge from Marinid restraint to Saadian opulence.
- **1578–1593 [Saadian]** — [[el-badi-palace]] in Marrakesh, built from the ransom of [[battle-of-three-kings]] and the gold of [[saadian-conquest-songhai]]. Stripped to its walls by Moulay Ismail in the 1690s; the ruin is the building.
- **1591–1603 [Saadian]** — [[saadian-tombs]] in Marrakesh, the necropolis of Ahmad al-Mansur and his family. Walled up under the Alaouites, rediscovered in 1917, now the most-visited Saadian site.
- **c. 1600 [Saadian]** — Bab Doukkala mosque and the Mouassine mosque-and-fountain complex in Marrakesh, both Saadian foundations.

## Alaouite Capitals (1666 – 1912)

- **1672–1727 [Alaouite]** — [[meknes]] under Moulay Ismail. New imperial capital: the Heri es-Souani granaries, the Bab Mansour (completed 1732), 40 km of walls, the royal stables, the Dar al-Makhzen palace complex. Stone re-used from the El-Badi.
- **1755 [Alaouite]** — Lisbon earthquake and tsunami damage Mogador, Salé, Rabat. Rebuilding sets the pattern for the next century.
- **1764–1769 [Alaouite]** — [[essaouira]] (Mogador) laid out for Sidi Mohammed ben Abdellah by the French engineer Théodore Cornut. The only purpose-built European-grid Moroccan city before the Protectorate, and the only one with a Vauban-style sea-bastion plan.
- **c. 1800 [Alaouite-southern]** — High point of the southern *ksour* and *kasbah* tradition: [[ait-benhaddou]], Telouet, Skoura. Earthen-architecture estates of caïds and trans-Saharan merchants. Most surviving fabric is 18th–19th century.
- **1859–1873 [Alaouite]** — Bahia Palace begun in Marrakesh by the vizier Si Moussa, expanded by his son Ba Ahmed (1894–1900). The most elaborate non-royal palace of the period; the financial backbone is partly toshavim merchant capital from the south.
- **c. 1880 [Alaouite]** — Dar Si Said in Marrakesh, brother of Bahia. Now the museum of Moroccan crafts.

## Protectorate Period (1912 – 1956)

- **1912–1925 [Protectorate]** — [[lyautey]]'s urban doctrine: leave the medinas alone, build the *villes nouvelles* on adjacent virgin land. Casablanca, Rabat, Fes, Meknes, Marrakesh all acquire a parallel European city. The doctrine is preservationist by colonial standards and segregationist in practice.
- **1915–1922 [Protectorate]** — Henri Prost master-plans for Rabat and Casablanca. Wide boulevards, garden quarters, mauresque civic buildings.
- **1918–1930 [Mauresque / Art Deco]** — Casablanca's downtown built. Marius Boyer, Edmond Brion, Auguste Cadet, Hippolyte Delaporte. Cinéma Rialto (1930), Hôtel Lincoln (1916, ruined), Villa Carl Ficke (1934). The densest concentration of inter-war architecture south of the Mediterranean.
- **1930s [Mauresque]** — Mahkama du Pacha in Casablanca (1948, but designed in the late 1930s); Rabat railway station (1923); Banque du Maroc, Rabat (1930). State-mauresque as the official idiom.
- **1947–1956 [late Protectorate / early Modern]** — ATBAT-Afrique housing in Casablanca: Carrières Centrales (Sidi Othmane), Cité Verticale, Sémiramis. Georges Candilis, Shadrach Woods, Vladimir Bodiansky. The case study that founded Team X and broke with CIAM orthodoxy.

## Independent Morocco (1956 – present)

- **1961–1971 [Modern]** — Hassan II's Rabat: the Mausoleum of Mohammed V (1961–1971, by Vo Toan), opposite the Almohad Hassan Tower. Deliberate citation: independent Morocco rebuilds the unfinished Almohad project.
- **1986–1993 [Late-Hassani]** — [[hassan-ii-mosque]] in Casablanca. Architect Michel Pinseau. Built half over the Atlantic on a 200-metre minaret. The largest building program of independent Morocco; politically commissioned, popularly funded, technically extraordinary.
- **1990s–2010s [Heritage]** — Restoration cycle: Bou Inania, Ibn Danan synagogue, Slat al-Fassiyine, Bahia Palace, Saadian Tombs. Driven by UNESCO listings (Fes 1981, Marrakesh 1985, Aït Benhaddou 1987, Meknes 1996, Volubilis 1997, Essaouira 2001, Tetouan medina 1997, Rabat 2012).
- **2011 [Royal-modern]** — Grand Théâtre de Rabat, by Zaha Hadid (commissioned 2010, opened 2024). Bouregreg riverbank redevelopment. Mohammed VI Tower (Salé, completed 2022, 250 m). The new Rabat skyline.
- **2018 [Infrastructure]** — Al Boraq high-speed line, Tangier–Kenitra. New stations at Tangier-Ville (Youssef Melehi), Kenitra (Silvio d'Ascia), Rabat-Agdal. The infrastructural face of contemporary Morocco.
- **2023 [Earthquake]** — Al Haouz earthquake. Tinmel mosque collapses; many High Atlas earthen villages, including parts of Aït Benhaddou's hinterland, are damaged. State-led reconstruction in earthen architecture is the live architectural question of the present moment.

## Threads Worth Following

- **Earthen architecture.** Pisé and adobe from Volubilis through Marrakesh's walls to the kasbahs of the south to the 2023 reconstruction. → [[earthen-architecture-morocco]]
- **Andalusi transmission.** The ornamental program — zellij, muqarnas, carved stucco, cedar — moves between Iberia and Morocco in both directions across all periods. → [[andalusi-inheritance]]
- **The medina–ville-nouvelle pair.** Lyautey's doctrine still structures every major Moroccan city. → [[lyautey]]
- **Royal commission as architectural engine.** From Idris II to Mohammed VI, the largest buildings are royal. The exceptions (al-Qarawiyyin, Bahia, the Pallache and Corcos houses) are diagnostic.
