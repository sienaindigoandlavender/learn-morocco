# Entry Template

Each timeline entry should follow a consistent shape. This is a working template — adjust based on what feels natural after writing a few.

```
# [Title]

[Date range or single date]
[Entry type: period / dynasty / event]

## What It Was

[2–4 sentences. The core decoding. State what's opaque to a casual reader and clarify it. Specific names, specific places, specific dates.]

## Why It Matters

[2–4 sentences. The downstream consequences. What this period/dynasty/event made possible, prevented, or shifted. Connection to other entries.]

## What Survives

[2–4 sentences. What's still visible today — buildings, traditions, language, place names, cultural practices. This is where the historical entry connects to the architectural and cultural entries readers will encounter.]

## Reading Further

[Short list of cross-references using [[wiki-link]] syntax. 3–6 links to related entries.]

## Sources

[1–3 authoritative sources you've consulted. Even private notes benefit from this — it forces honesty about what you actually know vs. what you're hand-waving.]
```

This template keeps each entry to 1–2 pages, forces decoding, and creates the cross-link structure naturally.

## Operational notes

- **Length.** 1–2 pages. If a period feels like it needs more, that's a sign it should be multiple entries.
- **Cross-linking.** Be generous. Every reference to another period, dynasty, building, person, or place that has its own entry should link to it. The wiki gets stronger as link density rises.
- **Sources.** Even in private notes, cite. The act of citing forces you to track what you actually know vs. what you're synthesizing from imperfect memory.
- **Voice.** Stay in the gossip-not-lessons register, even in private. Future-you reads your own wiki and benefits from the voice being already-trained on the right register.
- **The Amazigh substrate.** Most mainstream histories of Morocco subordinate the Amazigh layer to the Arab-dynastic narrative. The Slow Morocco frame foregrounds it. When you write the Almoravid entry, the lead is "the Almoravids were a Berber Sanhaja confederation," not "the Almoravids were a Muslim dynasty." When you write Pre-Islamic Morocco, the lead is the Amazigh world, not the Roman colonial layer that sat on top of it. Editorial position, not just chronology.
