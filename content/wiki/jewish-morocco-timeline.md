# Jewish Morocco — Dated Timeline

A chronological cut through two and a half thousand years, with the two threads — the ancient *toshavim* line and the post-1492 *megorashim* line — running side by side. Dates marked **[T]** belong to the toshavim story, **[M]** to the megorashim, **[B]** to both.

For the analytical entries see [[jewish-morocco]], [[jewish-morocco-toshavim]], and [[jewish-morocco-megorashim]].

---

## The Ancient Line (antiquity – 1492)

### Phoenician and Roman antiquity

- **c. 800–146 BCE [T]** — Punic period. Carthaginian trading posts at Lixus, Mogador, Tingis. Plausible first Jewish merchant presence alongside Phoenician traders, though the documentary trail is thin.
- **70 CE [T]** — Destruction of the Second Temple in Jerusalem. First well-attested wave of Jewish refugees and slaves to the western Mediterranean, including Mauretania Tingitana.
- **135 CE [T]** — Bar Kokhba revolt suppressed. A second, larger dispersal westward. Jewish inscriptions at [[volubilis]] date from this period and after.
- **c. 200–400 CE [T]** — Jewish communities documented across Roman Mauretania. Volubilis, Sala, Tingis. Burial inscriptions in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin.

### Late antiquity and the Berber-Jewish substrate

- **c. 400–600 CE [T]** — Communities consolidate in the Atlas, the Sous, and the Drâa. Conversion of whole Amazigh tribes to Judaism is reported by later Arab chroniclers — the Jarawa, the Nefzawa, the Fendelawa.
- **c. 690s [T]** — [[al-kahina]], the semi-legendary Jewish-Berber queen of the Aurès, leads the Jarawa resistance against the Umayyad armies of Hassan ibn al-Nu'man. Her defeat marks the beginning of the Arab-Islamic conquest of the Maghreb.

### Early Islamic centuries

- **788 CE [T]** — Foundation of the [[the-idrisids|Idrisid]] state. Jewish craftsmen and merchants invited into [[founding-of-fes|Fes]] from its earliest days. Idris II's city plan reportedly allotted a Jewish quarter near the Andalusian bank.
- **9th–11th c. [T]** — The toshavim become central to the [[trans-saharan-trade]] in gold and salt. Sijilmasa, on the desert edge, is partly a Jewish entrepôt. Letters from the Cairo Geniza document Moroccan Jewish merchants reaching Egypt, Yemen, and India.
- **1062 [T]** — Foundation of Marrakesh by the [[the-almoravids|Almoravids]]. Jews initially excluded from the city itself; settle in Aghmat-Ourika nearby.

### Almohad rupture and recovery

- **1146–1147 [T]** — Almohad conquest of Marrakesh and Fes. Forced conversions, executions, mass flight. The Andalusi-Jewish philosopher [[maimonides]] flees Córdoba to Fes around 1160, then on to Egypt by 1166. The single sharpest break in the toshavim story.
- **1212 [T]** — Almohad defeat at Las Navas de Tolosa. The dynasty begins to retreat; Jewish life resumes, quietly, under successor regimes.
- **1269 [T]** — [[the-marinids]] take Fes. Marinid sultans rebuild Jewish institutions and protect a class of Jewish court financiers and physicians.

### The first mellah

- **1438 [T]** — Foundation of the [[mellah]] in Fes. The Jewish community is moved from the medina to a new walled quarter beside the royal palace, on land that had been a salt marsh (*mellah* = "salt"). The Moroccan walled-quarter institution begins here, half a century before the Sephardic exiles arrive to fill its successors elsewhere.

## The Post-1492 Line (1492 – present)

### Iberian rupture and arrival

- **31 March 1492 [M]** — Alhambra Decree. Ferdinand and Isabella give the Jews of Castile and Aragon four months to convert or leave. Tens of thousands cross the Strait of Gibraltar to Morocco.
- **1492–1493 [M]** — Wattasid Morocco admits the refugees. Famine, plague, and a fire that destroys the Fes mellah in 1494 kill many. Some return to Iberia and convert; some continue east to Ottoman lands; the rest stay.
- **1497 [M]** — Manuel I of Portugal forcibly converts the Jewish refugees who had fled there from Spain. Over the next century, [[conversos]] and their descendants slip back to open Judaism by re-emigrating, mostly to northern Morocco.
- **c. 1500–1550 [M]** — Megorashim communities form in Fes, Tetouan, Salé, Larache, Asilah. They establish their own synagogues, rabbinical courts, and cemeteries, separate from the toshavim already in those cities. The *minhag al-megorashim* (Sephardic rite) gradually displaces the older toshavim rite in the urban north.

### Saadian century

- **1554–1659 [M]** — Under [[the-saadians]], Jewish merchants of megorashim descent become diplomatic and commercial intermediaries with Iberia, Italy, the Netherlands, and England. The Pallache family of Fes — diplomats and privateers — operates between Marrakesh, the Hague, and London.
- **1578 [B]** — [[battle-of-three-kings]]. Saadian victory funded in part by Jewish financiers in Fes and Marrakesh.
- **1591 [T]** — Saadian conquest of Songhai ([[saadian-conquest-songhai]]). Toshavim merchants of the southern oases are deeply implicated in the gold flow that follows.

### Alaouite consolidation

- **1666 [B]** — [[the-alaouites]] take power. Jewish court financiers continue under Moulay Rashid and especially under Moulay Ismail (r. 1672–1727), whose treasury depends on Jewish *negociadores*.
- **1764 [M]** — Sultan Sidi Mohammed ben Abdellah founds Mogador ([[founding-of-essaouira|Essaouira]]) and recruits ten elite megorashim merchant families — the *tujjar al-sultan* — to settle there and run European trade.
- **1790–1792 [B]** — Reign of Moulay Yazid. Pogroms in Tetouan, Fes, and Meknes. The single worst episode for both communities under the Alaouites; ends with the sultan's death.

### Nineteenth-century opening

- **1860 [B]** — Spanish-Moroccan war. Spanish occupation of Tetouan (1860–1862) opens the Tetouani megorashim to renewed direct Iberian contact.
- **1862 [B]** — Alliance Israélite Universelle opens its first Moroccan school in Tetouan. AIU schools become the principal vehicle by which Moroccan Jews — both lines — enter European modernity, French and Spanish-speaking.
- **1864 [B]** — *Dahir* of Mohammed IV, prompted by Moses Montefiore, formally reaffirms protection of Jewish subjects. Largely symbolic; the framework of [[dhimmi-status]] persists until the Protectorate.
- **1880 [B]** — Madrid Conference regularizes the European *protégé* system, under which thousands of Moroccan Jews acquire foreign protection — and, with it, exit from sultanic jurisdiction.

### Protectorate years

- **1912 [B]** — [[treaty-of-fes]]. French and Spanish [[french-protectorate|protectorates]] established. Anti-Jewish riots in the Fes mellah (the *Tritl*) during the chaotic transition kill roughly 60 Jews and destroy much of the quarter.
- **1912–1956 [B]** — Migration from the southern toshavim communities (Drâa, Sous, Atlas) to Casablanca, which grows from a few thousand Jews in 1900 to roughly 75,000 by 1950. Casablanca becomes the demographic center for the first time.
- **1940–1942 [B]** — Vichy racial laws applied in the French zone. Sultan [[mohammed-v]] refuses to hand over Moroccan Jews; the laws are implemented but no deportations take place. The myth and the partial truth of royal protection date from here.
- **1948 [B]** — Founding of Israel. First mass departures begin, especially from the poorer toshavim communities of the south. Roughly 250,000 Jews in Morocco at this point.

### Independence and emigration

- **1956 [B]** — [[independence-1956]]. Morocco joins the Arab League; emigration to Israel formally restricted but quietly tolerated through Operation Yachin (1961–1964), which moves roughly 100,000 Jews out via clandestine networks.
- **1961 [B]** — Sinking of the *Egoz* off the Rif coast. Forty-four Moroccan Jewish emigrants drown attempting clandestine departure. Trigger for the formalization of Operation Yachin under Hassan II.
- **1967 [B]** — Six-Day War. Second mass wave of departures, this time more from the urban megorashim communities of Casablanca, Fes, Tangier, Tetouan. By 1970 the community is below 35,000.
- **1970s–1990s [B]** — Slow continuous emigration to France, Israel, Spain, Venezuela, Canada. The community contracts to roughly 5,000 by 1990.
- **2003 [B]** — Casablanca bombings target, among others, a Jewish community center and a Jewish cemetery. The community, by then around 3,000, retracts further.
- **2020 [B]** — Abraham Accords; Morocco normalizes relations with Israel. State investment in Jewish heritage sites accelerates: Bayt Dakira in Essaouira, restoration of the Slat al-Fassiyine synagogue in Fes, the Casablanca Museum of Moroccan Judaism.
- **today [B]** — Roughly 2,000 Jews live in Morocco, almost all in Casablanca. Around 1 million Israelis and several hundred thousand French and Canadians claim Moroccan-Jewish descent.
