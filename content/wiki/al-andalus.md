# Al-Andalus

*al-Andalus* was the name Arab and Berber chroniclers gave to the Muslim-ruled portion of the Iberian peninsula — from the conquest of 711 to the surrender of Granada in 1492. The boundary moved across that span. At its 720s peak it ran along the Pyrenees and into Septimania. By 1300 it was a southern coastal strip from Tarifa to Almería. The story is essentially one long retreat from north to south, with two interventions from the Maghreb (Almoravid 1086, Almohad 1147) that bought another century and a half each time.

For the cross-strait dimension specifically — the troops, scholars, refugees, and architectural ideas that moved between Iberia and Morocco — see [[andalus-maghreb-connections]].

## Conquest (711 – 750)

- **710** — Tarif ibn Malik raids the south Iberian coast from Tangier; lands at the place that still bears his name (Tarifa).
- **711 [Tariq's invasion]** — [[tariq-ibn-ziyad|Tariq ibn Ziyad]], an Amazigh general of the Awraba, lands at the rock that takes his name (Jabal Tariq → Gibraltar) with c. 7,000 mostly Berber troops. Defeats King Roderic at the Battle of Guadalete (July 711). Takes Toledo by year's end.
- **712–714** — Musa ibn Nusayr arrives with Arab reinforcements. Zaragoza falls 714. The Visigothic state effectively dissolves.
- **716–720** — Conquest pushes north of the Pyrenees into Septimania (Narbonne 720).
- **732** — Battle of Tours / Poitiers. Charles Martel halts Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi's raid into Aquitaine. The northward push stops; the boundary stabilizes south of the Pyrenees.
- **740–743 [Berber Revolt]** — Maysara al-Matghari leads the great Amazigh revolt against Umayyad tax discrimination (see [[amazigh-timeline]]). The Andalusi Berber troops rebel; Arab–Berber civil war reshapes the new province.

## Emirate of Córdoba (756 – 929)

- **750 [Abbasid Revolution]** — The Umayyads of Damascus are massacred. One survivor, **Abd al-Rahman**, escapes via North Africa.
- **756 [The Falcon of Quraysh]** — Abd al-Rahman I lands in al-Andalus, defeats the governor at Musara, and proclaims himself emir at Córdoba. The independent Umayyad state of al-Andalus begins.
- **785** — Construction of the **Great Mosque of Córdoba** begins.
- **818 [The rabadī uprising]** — Revolt of the southern suburb of Córdoba against al-Hakam I. Crushed; the entire population is exiled. Many sail for Egypt and eventually take Crete (824); others go to Fez and settle the *'Adwat al-Andalus*, the Andalusi quarter that gives Fez its second great mosque.
- **822–852** — Abd al-Rahman II. Court culture flourishes; the Córdoban Maliki school is consolidated.
- **9th c.** — Long muwallad and *muladi* rebellions against the Umayyad emirate (Ibn Hafsun in the south, the Banu Qasi in the Ebro valley). The state nearly disintegrates.

## Caliphate of Córdoba (929 – 1031)

- **912–961** — **Abd al-Rahman III** restores central authority. **929**: declares the caliphate — the only successful Umayyad reply to the Abbasid claim. Founds **Madīnat al-Zahrā** (936) as the new caliphal capital. The largest, wealthiest, most populous polity in early-medieval Europe; estimated population of Córdoba in the 10th century: 250,000–500,000.
- **961–976 [al-Hakam II]** — The library reportedly holds 400,000 volumes. Final expansion of the Córdoba mosque (the maqsura and mihrab additions).
- **978–1002 [al-Mansur]** — The chamberlain Ibn Abi Amir, called *al-Mansur* ('the Victorious'), runs the state in the name of the child caliph Hisham II. **52 raids** into the Christian north. Sack of Barcelona (985), León (988), and Santiago de Compostela (997, with the bells of the cathedral carried to Córdoba on Christian shoulders).
- **1009–1031 [Fitna]** — The Berber civil wars after al-Mansur's death wreck the caliphate. Madīnat al-Zahrā sacked 1010. The last caliph deposed 1031.

## Taifa Kingdoms (1031 – 1086)

- The caliphate dissolves into roughly **30 taifa kingdoms** along ethnic and regional lines: the Abbadids of Seville, the Dhul-Nunids of Toledo, the Hudids of Zaragoza, the Aftasids of Badajoz, the Zirids of Granada, the Amirids of Valencia, the smaller emirates of Almería, Málaga, Murcia, Mallorca, Niebla, and others.
- **Cultural golden age**: courts compete for poets, scientists, philosophers. Ibn Hazm (994–1064), the polymath of Córdoba; Ibn Zaydun, the elegist of the lost caliphate; Ibn Gabirol and Samuel ha-Nagid in the Jewish scholarly traditions.
- **Military catastrophe**: the taifas pay protection money (*parias*) to Christian kings. By 1085 Castile and León collect more revenue from al-Andalus than from their own subjects.
- **1085 [Toledo falls]** — Alfonso VI of León-Castile takes Toledo. Loss of one of the great Andalusi cities; psychological breaking point. The Abbadid king of Seville, al-Mu'tamid, sends to Marrakesh for the Almoravids.

## Almoravid Andalus (1086 – 1147)

- **1086 [Sagrajas / Zallāqa]** — Yusuf ibn Tashfin crosses from Ceuta and destroys Alfonso VI at Sagrajas, 23 October. Returns to Marrakesh.
- **1090–1094** — Yusuf returns; deposes the taifa kings one by one. By 1094 al-Andalus is annexed to the Almoravid empire.
- **1094–1102** — **Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar (El Cid)** holds Valencia as an independent lordship; recovered by the Almoravids on his death.
- **1118 [Zaragoza]** — Falls to Alfonso I of Aragon. Aragonese push into the Ebro valley.
- **1140s** — Almoravid authority collapses under doctrinal critique from the rising Almohads. **Second Taifa period** briefly emerges (Ibn Mardanish in Murcia, the Hudids in Zaragoza).
- **1147** — Lisbon falls to the Second Crusade in October. The Almohads take Seville the same year.

## Almohad Andalus (1147 – 1212)

- **1147–1172** — The Almohads consolidate al-Andalus, fighting Ibn Mardanish ('the Wolf King') of Murcia until his death (1172).
- **1172 [Great Mosque of Seville]** — Construction begins. The mosque whose minaret is the Giralda. See [[almohad-architecture]].
- **1184–1199 [Yacoub al-Mansour]** — The Almohad apex. **Battle of Alarcos (1195)** crushes Castile.
- **1212 [Las Navas de Tolosa]** — 16 July. Combined armies of Castile, Aragon, Navarre, and Portugal break the caliph al-Nasir. The hinge of the Reconquista.

## The Reconquista Endgame (1212 – 1248)

- **1229 [Mallorca]** — James I of Aragon takes the island. End of Almohad Balearics.
- **1230–1243 [Murcia]** — Submitted as a Castilian protectorate; annexed 1266.
- **1236 [Córdoba]** — Falls to Ferdinand III. The Great Mosque becomes the cathedral.
- **1238 [Valencia]** — Falls to James I of Aragon.
- **1238 [Granada]** — Muhammad I founds the **Nasrid emirate** at Granada — the last Muslim state in Iberia.
- **1248 [Seville]** — Falls to Ferdinand III. The Almohad Great Mosque becomes the cathedral; the Giralda becomes the bell tower; the Alcázar passes into Castilian use.
- **1249 [Algarve]** — Faro and the Algarve fall to Portugal. The Reconquista is functionally complete except for Granada.
- **1262 [Niebla]** — The last independent taifa-style emirate in the southwest falls to Castile.

## Nasrid Granada (1238 – 1492)

- **1246** — Muhammad I becomes Castilian vassal; pays annual tribute. The arrangement holds, more or less, for two and a half centuries.
- **1275–1340** — Six Marinid interventions from Morocco prop up the Nasrids. The last, the **Battle of Río Salado (30 October 1340)**, is a Castilian-Portuguese-Aragonese victory; no Maghrebi army crosses the strait again until the 20th century.
- **1333–1354 [Yusuf I]** and **1354–1359 [Muhammad V]** — The Alhambra reaches its current form. Comares Palace, Court of the Lions.
- **1462** — Gibraltar falls to Castile. The strait passes into Christian hands.
- **1482–1492 [Granada War]** — The Catholic Monarchs Ferdinand and Isabella press south. Málaga falls 1487 (population enslaved); Almería 1489.
- **2 January 1492** — **Boabdil surrenders Granada**. The Treaty of Granada (Capitulations) guarantees the Muslim population freedom of religion and property.

## Mudéjar and Morisco (1492 – 1614)

- **1499–1502** — The Capitulations are repudiated. Cardinal Cisneros's forced conversions in Granada; revolt of the Albaicín (1499) and the Alpujarras (1500–1501); Pragmatic of 1502 ordering all Castilian Muslims to convert or leave. The converted are now **Moriscos**.
- **1525–1526** — Forced conversions extended to Aragon and Valencia.
- **1568–1571 [War of the Alpujarras]** — Morisco rebellion in the Sierra Nevada under Aben Humeya. Crushed by Don John of Austria; Granadan Moriscos forcibly dispersed across Castile.
- **1609–1614 [Expulsion]** — Philip III decrees the expulsion of all Moriscos. Roughly **300,000** people — perhaps 4% of Spain's population — forced out. Most go to North Africa: Salé (the Hornacheros), Tetouan, Rabat, Fez, Tlemcen, Tunis. End of the Andalusi presence in Iberia.

## Threads Worth Following

- **The Maghrebi rescues.** Almoravid 1086 and Almohad 1147 both extend Andalusi survival by a century. Both are Amazigh dynasties with their political center in Morocco and their cultural-economic peripheries in Iberia. → [[andalus-maghreb-connections]]
- **The architectural inheritance.** The Cordoban tradition (8th–10th c.) is the source from which Almoravid and Almohad architecture both descend. The Mudéjar art of Christian Spain is its direct continuation. → [[almohad-architecture]]
- **The Sephardic dimension.** Andalusi Jewry — Hasdai ibn Shaprut, Samuel ha-Nagid, Maimonides — is structurally inseparable from the Andalusi story. The 1492 Alhambra Decree expelled both Muslim and Jewish populations on the same logic.
- **The Berber substrate.** The 711 conquest army was mostly Amazigh; the 740 revolt was Amazigh; the Almoravid and Almohad rescues were Amazigh. Al-Andalus is, demographically, an Amazigh as much as an Arab story. → [[amazigh-timeline]]
